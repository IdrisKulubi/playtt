# Product

## Register

product

## Users

PlayTT is optimized for **first-time visitors** who must understand the offer within one viewport and trust the product before they commit to a booking.

Secondary audiences still matter, but they follow the first-time path:

- **Casual groups** booking a private pod with friends (often one coordinator on mobile)
- **Returning players** who want a fast repeat booking with minimal explanation

Typical context: **on the go**, on a phone, with short attention between classes or after work. Copy and step density should assume one-thumb use, not desktop planning sessions.

## Product Purpose

PlayTT is a premium, self-serve booking product for private table tennis sessions. It exists to move a player from discovery to a confirmed reservation with very little friction, while keeping the experience calm, clear, and trustworthy.

The product is built around one primary journey:

1. Understand what PlayTT is
2. Pick a location
3. See open timing availability
4. Set group size
5. Review a clear checkout summary
6. Complete payment and access later phases

Every major screen should support that journey. If a screen does not help the player move toward a booking, it should be quiet and secondary.

## Brand Personality

**Premium consumer calm**: high-trust, human, and confident without hype. Think Oura or Apple Fitness+ in restraint and polish, not sports gamification or startup pitch energy.

Voice and tone:

- Concise and action-first
- Clear labels over clever copy
- Calm confidence over sports hype
- Never robotic, generic, or startup-pitchy

Visual personality is defined in DESIGN.md. Strategically: distinctly blue-led, premium and private, warm enough to feel human, never flashy.

## Anti-references

PlayTT should not look or behave like:

- **Generic SaaS landing pages**: hero metrics, three identical feature cards, social-proof walls, restated headings
- **Gamified sports apps**: streaks, badges, neon gradients, loud celebration, competitive noise
- **AI-tool dashboards**: decorative glass everywhere, purple gradients, chat-first chrome, slop layouts
- **Budget booking sites**: cluttered filters, tiny type, anxious urgency timers, trust-eroding density
- **Empty minimalism**: whitespace without hierarchy, interfaces that feel unfinished rather than precise

## Design Principles

- Start with the player's real decision, not the system's internal structure
- Show less, but make each step more legible
- Use strong hierarchy so the next action is always obvious
- Preserve trust through pricing clarity, stable layout, and consistent controls
- Keep mobile behavior first-class, not compressed desktop
- Practice what you preach: the booking flow itself should demonstrate product quality

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AA** as the baseline bar:

- Strong contrast on dark surfaces
- Visible focus states using the primary ring color
- Keyboard-accessible controls and logical focus order
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for all non-essential animation
- Do not rely on color alone for critical state or errors

Mobile booking is the default context: touch targets and one-thumb flows should meet AA expectations in practice, not only on paper.

## Success Standard

A first-time visitor should feel PlayTT is a real premium product within a few seconds. A player entering the booking flow should feel guided by the interface, not asked to figure it out. Returning players should be able to rebook quickly once they know the product. All three outcomes matter equally for the current build.
